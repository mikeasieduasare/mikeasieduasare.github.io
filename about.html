About

About

I’m Michael Asiedu Asare, an AI safety researcher, engineer, and builder at the Biomedical Technologies Lab, Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST), in Kumasi, Ghana.

01

Background

Trained as a biomedical engineer. Now studying how AI systems fail.

I completed a BSc in Biomedical Engineering at KNUST with First-Class Honours. My thesis, a Sinkhorn-based optimal transport framework for breast cancer diagnosis, was graded A.

Since 2024 I have been a Graduate Research Fellow at the Biomedical Technologies Lab. My first papers there were in medical machine learning: multimodal diagnosis, lung CT classification, and fall detection. Each was built for resource-constrained settings, where missing data, changing conditions, and limited hardware are normal.

Designing for those conditions led to the question that now defines my research. How does an AI system’s reliability wear down when its conditions change, and can we detect that before it turns into failure?

03

Current interests

Research agenda →

Robustness, reliability, and evaluation under distribution shift.

That is where my current work sits. Around it are the areas I intend to grow into: verification and formal methods, AI security, interpretability, alignment and controllability, and the practice of deploying AI systems safely.

04

Building & teaching

Research that other people can use, and people who can use it.

I co-founded TPL Innovations, a student learning initiative that ran STEM workshops and peer-learning sessions for more than 100 students at KNUST, and helped draft the charter for Ghana Society of Biomedical Engineers student chapters across five universities.

As a teaching assistant I have taught introductory and intermediate modules in AI for healthcare, machine learning foundations, and responsible AI to more than 80 students.
