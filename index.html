AI safety · Reliability · Deployment

Building AI systems we can trust.

I study how AI systems lose reliability when the world around them changes. The goal is to catch that decline early enough to raise a warning, report uncertainty honestly, and fail safely instead of silently.

01

Research

Full research agenda →

How do changes in a learned system’s inputs, environment, internal state, or computational structure affect the properties that support reliable behavior, and can those changes be detected or prevented before they produce failure?

  1. 01 Current focus

    Robustness & Reliability

    How model behavior changes under distribution shift and compression, and whether uncertainty and calibration still mean what they claim.

  2. 02

    Verification & Formal Methods

    Specifications, invariants, and evidence that can be checked before deployment rather than inferred after failure.

  3. 03

    AI Security

    The attack surfaces of learned systems, and how they behave under deliberate rather than accidental pressure.

  4. 04

    Interpretability

    Internal representations as evidence about what a model is doing, not only what it outputs.

  5. 05

    Alignment & Controllability

    Whether systems keep pursuing the intended objective, and remain correctable, as their conditions change.

  6. 06

    Safe Deployment

    Monitoring, abstention, and fallback: mechanisms that let a system fail visibly and safely instead of silently.

Selected work

All projects →
  1. Early warning May–Sep 2026

    Early Warning of Clinical AI Failure Under Distribution Shift

    Introduces Warning Lead Time, a measure of how much notice a monitor gives before a model’s performance falls below an agreed failure threshold. Across 36 runs, whether a model failed depended on the type of shift it faced, not just its architecture.

    Lead researcher · Manuscript under review

  2. Compression Jul–Sep 2026

    Model Reliability Under Compression

    A case study on mpox. Quantization and pruning make models cheaper to run, but what do they do beyond headline accuracy? This work examines calibration, abstention, conformal prediction, and the full predictive distribution.

    Manuscript under review

  3. Safety evaluation Apr–Jun 2026

    G-MASS

    An open-source evaluation protocol testing whether the safety behavior of medical AI assistants holds across Twi and Ghanaian English. Co-developed the Safety Degradation Score, a measure of cross-lingual safety drift.

    Clinical AI safety lead · 3rd of 104, Africa AI Safety Prize

03

Building

Research has to survive contact with deployment, so the work ships as things other people can run and test.

  • Open evaluation

    The G-MASS probe set, released publicly so others can test cross-lingual safety behavior in medical assistants.

  • Models sized for deployment

    Contributed to a 0.312 MB fall detector that runs on-device in 7.07 ms per sample; co-developed a 3.33 MB lung CT classifier that needs no specialized hardware.

  • Learning infrastructure

    Co-founded TPL Innovations (2022–2025), a student initiative that ran STEM workshops and peer learning for 100+ students at KNUST.

06

About

From biomedical engineering to technical AI safety.

I am a Graduate Research Fellow at the Biomedical Technologies Lab, Kwame Nkrumah University of Science and Technology (KNUST), where I also completed a BSc in Biomedical Engineering with First-Class Honours.

My early work was in medical machine learning: combining data sources for breast cancer diagnosis, and building compact models for lung CT and fall detection. One question kept coming back in that work. What happens when a deployed model meets conditions it was not built for? That question is now the center of my research.
