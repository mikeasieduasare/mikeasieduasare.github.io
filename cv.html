Research interests

I study how changes in a learned system’s inputs, environment, internal state, and computational structure affect the properties that support reliable behavior. My current research focuses on robustness, reliability, and evaluation under distribution shift. I am also interested in AI verification and formal methods, AI security, interpretability, alignment and controllability, and safe and reliable AI deployment.

Early Warning of Clinical AI Failure Under Distribution Shift

May–Sep 2026

Lead Researcher

  • Introduced Warning Lead Time, a metric for how much warning a monitor gives before model performance crosses a prespecified failure threshold. Evaluated it in a 2×2 factorial design (Fourier Domain Adaptation vs. linear blending; chest radiography vs. histopathology) across ResNet18, DenseNet121, and ViT-B/16 (36 runs).
  • Showed that robustness depends on the type of shift, not just the architecture: 1 of 18 runs failed under FDA versus 18 of 18 under linear blending. In radiography, the architecture ranking inverted between mechanisms.
  • Tested a critical-slowing-down precursor from complex-systems theory: lag-1 autocorrelation rose before failure in only 10 of 19 failures (53%, p = 1.00), no better than chance.
  • Under review at Artificial Intelligence in Medicine.

Lightweight Bi-LSTM–TCN for Lung Cancer Diagnosis

Jun–Nov 2025

Researcher

  • Co-developed a hybrid Bi-LSTM–TCN classifier with efficient channel attention and depthwise separable convolutions for three-class CT classification.
  • Added LIME and SHAP explanations and analyzed the seven test-set errors, including the single missed malignancy.
  • 98.06% accuracy and 99.88% AUC on a held-out test set with 279,561 parameters (3.33 MB).
  • Second-author publication, Biomedical Engineering and Computational Biology (2026).

Optimal Transport for Breast Cancer Diagnostics

May–Aug 2025

Lead Researcher

  • Formulated multimodal late fusion as an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem, combining a histopathology classifier (EfficientNetB0, BreakHis) and a clinical-risk model (LightGBM, BCSC) trained on separate, unpaired cohorts.
  • Designed a clinically weighted cost matrix that penalizes missed cancers more heavily than false alarms.
  • Under simulated modality dropout, recall stayed at 0.96–1.00 while PR-AUC fell from 0.775 to 0.716 at 50% dropout; outperformed averaging, max-rule, and logistic-regression fusion on PR-AUC.
  • Supported by Google’s TensorFlow College Outreach Award to the Biomedical Technologies Lab.
  • First-author publication, Cancer Informatics (2026).

Lightweight Attention Models for Fall Detection
