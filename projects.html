AI safety & reliability

  1. Early warning May–Sep 2026 Lead researcher

    Early Warning of Clinical AI Failure Under Distribution Shift

    Problem
    A monitor is only useful if its warning arrives before the model fails. How much advance notice do monitoring signals actually give?
    Method
    Introduced Warning Lead Time, a measure of how much warning a monitor gives before model performance falls below a prespecified failure threshold. Tested it in a 2×2 design that crossed two types of shift (Fourier Domain Adaptation and linear blending) with two imaging domains (chest radiography and histopathology), across ResNet18, DenseNet121, and ViT-B/16: 36 runs in total. Also tested “critical slowing down”, an early-warning signal borrowed from complex-systems theory.
    Result
    Robustness depended on the type of shift, not just the architecture: 1 of 18 runs failed under FDA, compared with 18 of 18 under linear blending. In radiography, the ranking of architectures flipped between the two shift types. ViT-B/16 was the least robust under FDA and the most robust under linear blending. The critical-slowing-down signal (lag-1 autocorrelation) rose before only 10 of 19 failures (53%, p = 1.00), no better than chance.

    Under review at Artificial Intelligence in Medicine · Presented at BioMedEng UK, September 2026

  3. Safety evaluation Apr–Jun 2026 Clinical AI safety lead

    G-MASS

    Problem
    Does a medical AI assistant stay safe in the languages its users actually speak? Here, that means Twi and Ghanaian English.
    Method
    Co-designed an open-source evaluation protocol for medical foundation models in low-resource languages, and developed probes testing whether safety behavior holds across both languages. Co-developed the Safety Degradation Score (SDS), a metric quantifying cross-lingual safety drift.
    Result
    The set of 300 probes is publicly released on Hugging Face. The team placed 3rd of 104 in the Africa AI Safety Prize (Centre for AI Security and Access).

    Complete · Team project

Biomedical machine learning

  1. Multimodal fusion May–Aug 2025 Lead researcher

    Resilient Multimodal Diagnosis: Optimal Transport for Breast Cancer

    Problem
    Clinical data arrive asynchronous, unpaired, or incomplete. Can a diagnostic model combine modalities that were never collected together, and stay reliable when one is missing?
    Method
    Formulated multimodal late fusion as an entropy-regularized optimal transport problem, combining a histopathology classifier (EfficientNetB0, BreakHis) with a clinical-risk model (LightGBM, BCSC) trained on separate, unpaired cohorts. A clinically weighted cost matrix penalizes missed cancers more heavily than false alarms.
    Result
    Under simulated modality dropout, recall stayed at 0.96–1.00 while PR-AUC fell from 0.775 to 0.716 at 50% dropout. The learned transport plan let clinical risk upgrade a benign biopsy prediction but rarely downgrade a malignant one. Outperformed averaging, max-rule, and logistic-regression fusion on PR-AUC.

    Published · Cancer Informatics, 2026 · First author

    doi:10.1177/11769351261420789 ↗

  2. Medical imaging Jun–Nov 2025 Researcher

    Lightweight Bi-LSTM–TCN for Lung Cancer Diagnosis

    Problem
    Early lung cancer detection from low-dose CT with a model small enough to deploy where specialized hardware is unavailable.
    Method
    Co-developed a hybrid Bi-LSTM–TCN classifier with efficient channel attention and depthwise separable convolutions for three-class (normal / benign / malignant) CT classification, with LIME and SHAP explanations and an analysis of every test-set error.
    Result
    98.06% accuracy and 99.88% AUC on a held-out test set with 279,561 parameters (3.33 MB). The seven test-set errors, including the single missed malignancy, were examined individually.

    Published · Biomedical Engineering and Computational Biology, 2026 · Second author

    doi:10.1177/11795972261472208 ↗

  3. Wearable sensing Jan–Mar 2025 Researcher

    LiteFallNet: Real-Time Fall Detection

    Problem
    Fall detection that runs on-device from inertial sensors alone, with no camera feed or cloud upload.
    Method
    Contributed to a lightweight model combining a GRU, a temporal convolutional block, depthwise separable convolutions, and squeeze-and-excitation, evaluated on FallAllD and UMAFall with Grad-CAM and LIME explanations.
    Result
    17,751 parameters (0.312 MB), 7.07 ms per sample. The two failure types were reported separately rather than as accuracy alone: 1.45% of falls missed and 2.97% of daily activities falsely flagged on FallAllD, under instance-level splits.

    Published · Digital Health, 2025 · Third author

    doi:10.1177/20552076251386698 ↗
