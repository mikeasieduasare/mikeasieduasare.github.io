Research

Warning before failure.

My research aims to design and build AI systems that are safe, secure, and effective to deploy. It starts from one question: how does reliability wear down, and can we see it happening in time?

01

The question

How do changes in a learned system’s inputs, environment, internal state, or computational structure affect the properties that support reliable behavior, and can those changes be detected or prevented before they produce failure?

The question names four ways change can enter a deployed system. Each one can turn a model that passed its tests into a model that no longer deserves the trust it was given.

  1. Inputs

    The data a model sees drifts away from the data it was trained on.

    Studied: early warning under distribution shift

  2. Environment

    The same model is used in a new setting, population, or language.

    Studied: cross-lingual safety drift in G-MASS

  3. Internal state

    Representations inside the model change in ways its outputs may not yet reveal.

    Open: a direction for future work

  4. Computational structure

    Quantization and pruning alter the computation to make deployment cheaper.

    Studied: reliability under compression

Research areas

Six areas, each marked by how far my own work has gone into it. A current focus has papers or manuscripts behind it. A future interest is a direction I plan to pursue but have not yet published in.

  • Current focus Active work with manuscripts or publications
  • Related work Adjacent methods used in published work

01 Current focus

Robustness & Reliability

How a model’s behavior changes when its operating conditions change, and whether the confidence it reports can still be trusted when it matters most.

  • Distribution shift
  • Uncertainty
  • Calibration
  • Conformal prediction
  • Compression reliability
  • Monitoring
Work so far
  • Early warning of clinical AI failure under distribution shift (manuscript under review)
  • Model reliability under compression (manuscript under review)
  • Resilient multimodal diagnosis: staying reliable when a data source is missing (published 2026)
02 Future interest

Verification & Formal Methods

Evidence that can be checked before deployment. That means writing down what a system must do, then showing that it does it, instead of discovering its limits through failures.

  • Formal guarantees
  • Invariants
  • Specification
  • Verification
  • Pre-deployment evidence
Closest current work
  • Conformal prediction offers distribution-free coverage guarantees; the compression study examines whether they survive quantization and pruning.
03 Future interest

AI Security

The same question, asked about attackers instead of accidents: where AI systems can be attacked or compromised, and how they behave under deliberate pressure.

  • Adversarial behavior
  • System compromise
  • Model security
  • Attack surfaces
04 Related work

Interpretability

Treating a model’s internal representations as evidence about what it is doing, and asking whether that evidence changes before its outputs do.

  • Internal representations
  • Mechanistic understanding
  • Behavioral evidence
Related work
  • Post-hoc explanation (Grad-CAM, LIME, SHAP) in the fall detection and lung CT studies.
  • An interpretable optimal-transport cost matrix in the breast cancer fusion work.
05 Related work

Alignment & Controllability

Whether a system keeps pursuing the goal it was given, keeps behaving safely, and can still be corrected as its conditions drift away from the ones it was trained and tested in.

  • Objective robustness
  • Controllability
  • Safe behavior
  • Human intent
Related work
  • G-MASS tests whether the safety behavior of medical AI assistants holds when the language changes.
06 Current focus

Safe Deployment

The mechanisms that turn evidence into action: monitoring that raises a warning with time to spare, abstention when a model is out of its depth, and fallbacks that make failure visible rather than silent.

  • Monitoring
  • Abstention
  • Safe failure
  • Deployment evaluation
Work so far
  • Warning Lead Time, a measure of how much notice a monitor gives before failure, in the early-warning study.
  • Abstention behavior after compression, in the compression study.
  • Models sized for resource-constrained clinical settings, in the lung CT and fall detection studies.
